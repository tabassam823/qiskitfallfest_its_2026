Interactive Visual Qubit Playground

Instructions:

  • Click or click+drag to place "wires".
  • Place wires beside the source ( ) to connect the wire to the source. Once you place a wire beside a source, qubits ( ) will start to flow out of the source.
  • Press the 'x' key to place an (a.k.a. NOT, or diagonal reflection) gate under your cursor.
  • Press the 'z' key to place a (vertical reflection) gate under the cursor.
  • Press the delete/backspace key to remove the wire/gate under your cursor.

Advanced instructions

  • Press the 'h' key to place an (a.k.a. Hadamard) gate under the cursor.
  • Press the 'r' key to place an (a.k.a. R or 22.5° rotation) gate under the cursor.
    • 📘 Hint: try an H gate, followed by an R gate, followed by another H gate. What does this combination of gates do? How about H, R, R, H? H, R, R, R, H?
  • Press the 'm' key to place a measurement device under the cursor.
    • 📝 Note: the measurement device isn't useful for this puzzle, but it is included because it might be interesting to see what quantum measurement is like. The measurement device makes an input qubit either point up or right . The qubit aligns to up or right randomly, but the likelihood of the alignment result is proportional to how vertical or horizontal the qubit is (e.g. will most likely be aligned to pointing right).

About

The playground

This playground gives you an visual way to understand and interact with quantum computing. You can get a sense for how a qubit can look and how it can change. It also allows you to create some simple "quantum algorithms" to solve a puzzle. My goal with this project is to create the foundation for a visual and interactive way of trying out quantum computer simulations.

This playground uses a mix between conventional "quantum circuit" symbols, and custom symbols.

If you're curious about how the "arrow" qubits translate into conventional qubit notation:
=∣0⟩,=|0\rangle,=∣0⟩, =−∣0⟩,=-|0\rangle,=−∣0⟩,

=∣1⟩,=|1\rangle,=∣1⟩, =−∣1⟩,=-|1\rangle,=−∣1⟩,
=∣0⟩+∣1⟩2,...=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},...=2​∣0⟩+∣1⟩​,... (a qubit in superposition!)

Also note that in real quantum computers, measurements are the only way to get information out of a quantum computer! Notice how measurements change the qubit (by aligning it) and give a random (yet controllable) result.

What's next for interactive visual quantum?

I hope that this interactive and visual way of experiencing quantum computing can both make quantum computing more accessible, and set a foundation for a visual understanding of quantum computing.

I plan on making an article explaining the basics of quantum computing, with puzzles interspersed to challenge your understanding. Please follow me here on Observable or on twitter @elliotokay if you want to see more interactive visual quantum computing!

I would greatly appreciate any feedback on this playground and article! Please feel free to leave a comment or reach out, it would help me a lot!
