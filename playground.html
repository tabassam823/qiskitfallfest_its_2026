How Visual Qubit Vectors Work

This visual model translates the abstract mathematical notation of quantum computing into intuitive 2D geometric vectors and reflections.

|0⟩

Computational Basis States

The arrow direction indicates the probability amplitude vector:

  • • Pointing Right (→, Red) = \(|0\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}\)
  • • Pointing Left (←) = \(-|0\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}-1 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}\)
  • • Pointing Up (↑, Blue) = \(|1\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}\)
  • • Pointing Down (↓) = \(-|1\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ -1\end{pmatrix}\)
|+⟩

Quantum Superposition

When an arrow points diagonally (e.g. 45° or 225°), the qubit is in a linear combination of basis states:

\(|\psi\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}} = |+\rangle\)

The color smoothly transitions between red and blue according to the probability \(P(|0\rangle) = |\alpha|^2\).

📸

Quantum Measurement

Real quantum computers only yield information via projective measurement. When a qubit hits a 📸 device, it randomly collapses to either \(|0\rangle\) or \(|1\rangle\) with probability \(P = |\alpha|^2\).

Gates act as geometric rotations and reflections across axes on the state vector.
